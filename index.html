The AI-defined vehicle

From zonal architecture to a car that senses, thinks and learns

Miroslav Nikolic
October 2026

Today’s story in three steps: the zonal, software-defined architecture is the foundation; on top of it, edge AI in the car plus AI in the cloud make the vehicle AI-defined; the result is a car that recognizes, adapts to and anticipates its driver.

Architecture shift

Zonal architecture makes the car software-defined

Legacy cars add a controller per function. Zonal cars run features as software on one central computer.

Legacy 100+ single-purpose ECUs, point-to-point wiring

Zonal 4 zone controllers + 1 central computer

Updates over the air

Software is decoupled from hardware, so features, fixes and AI models ship OTA.

Less wiring, fewer boxes

Fewer controllers and a shorter harness cut weight, cost and assembly effort.

One computer sees everything

Central compute has all sensor data in one place — what AI features need.

CONFIDENTIAL
The legacy car grew one ECU per function — over a hundred boxes, each with its own firmware and supplier, tied together by point-to-point wiring and a gateway. Every message has to squeeze through that gateway. [Next: zonal]
The zonal car groups inputs and outputs by location into four zone controllers that connect over Ethernet to one central computer. Features live in central software and are updated over the air. Three benefits: OTA updates for the car’s lifetime, less wiring and hardware, and one computer with access to all data — exactly what AI needs.

Step 1, sense: cameras, radar, the infrared driver camera and the microphones report to the nearest of four zone controllers. [Next: think]
Step 2, think on board: the zones stream everything over Ethernet to the central computer, where edge AI fuses it and runs vision, speech and intent models in real time — offline if necessary, with raw data staying in the car. [Next: sync]
Step 3, sync: only masked events and embeddings go up to the cloud (gold). The cloud learns across the whole fleet and sends back user profiles, recommendations and updated models (pale green). [Next: personalize]
Step 4, personalize: the result reaches the driver through the infotainment and the voice assistant. Headlights on — this loop is what every use case runs on.

Use case 1

The car recognizes its driver

An infrared camera captures the face and edge AI turns it into an encrypted embedding. Only the embedding goes to the cloud to be matched.

CONFIDENTIAL
An infrared camera on the steering column films the driver’s face — it works day and night. Edge AI finds the face landmarks and turns them into a compact, encrypted embedding. Only that embedding, never the image, goes to the cloud identity service, which matches it against enrolled drivers and returns a profile ID. That ID triggers the next use case.

Use case 2

The profile follows the driver

Once the driver is identified, their profile comes down from the cloud and the whole car adjusts — in any car of the fleet.

CONFIDENTIAL
As soon as the driver is identified, the car pulls their profile from the cloud and applies it everywhere: seat and mirror memory, climate, media, saved destinations, drive mode and display preferences. The same profile follows the driver to any car in the fleet — a rental, a shared car or a new vehicle.

Use case 2, predictive intention

After five minutes, the car anticipates

Context from the first minutes places the driver in a behaviour cluster. A temporal recommender suggests the next step on screen and by voice.

CONFIDENTIAL
During the first five minutes the car quietly collects context: time, heading, media behaviour, cabin settings and driving style. Edge AI places the driver in a behavioural cluster — here, weekday commuters. A temporal recommendation engine ranks the most likely next need for that cluster at this time and place, and the car offers it through the infotainment and the voice assistant. Accept or dismiss becomes training feedback.

Use case 3, driver intention

Eyes off the screen, the screen dims

The IR camera tracks head pose and gaze vectors. When the driver looks away for more than two seconds the infotainment dims, and it brightens the moment the gaze returns.

CONFIDENTIAL
The same IR camera now tracks the eyes and head pose. Edge AI computes a gaze vector from each eye and checks whether it falls inside the infotainment screen zone. When the driver looks away for more than about two seconds, the display dims to around 30 percent — less glare at night, less pull on peripheral attention, lower power draw — and it returns to full brightness the moment the gaze comes back. Thresholds are illustrative.

Use case 4

A voice assistant that thinks in the car

Wake word, speech-to-text and a small language model all run on board, so answers never wait for the network. Optionally, masked logs go to the cloud for performance analysis and adapter retraining.

CONFIDENTIAL
The whole assistant runs in the car: microphones, wake word, speech-to-text and a small language model on the central computer. It reasons and answers on board, so it works without a network and audio never leaves the vehicle. The cloud is optional and sits outside the answer path: with consent, interaction logs are masked — names and places become tokens — and uploaded. The cloud uses them for performance analysis and to retrain the model’s adapters, and the improved adapter comes back over the air.
